# Hidden Pair

Suppose that in a row, column or box, two digits, say 4 and 8, can each go in only the same two cells: no other cell of the unit has 4 or 8 as a candidate. The unit needs a 4 and an 8, and these two cells are the only places for them, so one cell is 4 and the other is 8.

Those two cells are then full, so any other candidates in them can be removed.

Hidden pairs are harder to see than naked ones, because the extra candidates hide them. Look for two digits that can each go in the same two cells of a unit. Once the extras are removed, the hidden pair becomes a naked pair.
